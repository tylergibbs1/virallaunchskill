## Bold Claim Scorecard

Score each 1-10:

- **Novelty**: Makes the product feel like something new exists.
- **Pain relevance**: Ties to a problem the market already cares about.
- **Specificity**: Avoids broad category mush.
- **Defensibility**: Can be proven by demo, data, or credible evidence.
- **Memorability**: Easy to repeat after one read.

Reject claims that sound like:

- "AI [category] maker"
- "platform for modern teams"
- "streamlines workflows"
- "saves time with AI"
- "all-in-one solution"

## Hook Scorecard

Score each 1-10:

- **Immediate object**: Reader knows what launched.
- **Why it matters**: Stakes are obvious.
- **Why now/new**: It feels timely or newly possible.
- **Tension**: Creates curiosity, contrast, or conflict.
- **Plain language**: Clear to a non-expert.

Strong hook patterns:

- "We built [specific new thing] so [painful old behavior] dies."
- "[Old category] makes you do [bad workaround]. [Product] does [new behavior]."
- "For years, [audience] had to [pain]. Today we are launching [new mechanism]."
- "This looks like [familiar thing], but it actually [surprising capability]."

## Narrative Scorecard

The body/script should include:

- Before state: what is broken or accepted as normal.
- Product behavior: what changes on screen.
- Proof: why the claim is real.
- Stakes: why the viewer should care now.
- Expansion: what becomes possible if this works.
- CTA: what to do next.

## Weapons Check

Judge every line on:

- **Invention novelty**: Does this line make the product feel more new, important, or inevitable?
- **Copy intensity**: Does this line create a sharper feeling than a neutral description?

Cut or rewrite a line when:

- Any company in the category could say it.
- It repeats a prior point without raising stakes.
- It explains what the visual can show.
- It uses abstract praise instead of concrete behavior.
- It is true but boring.

Use this table:

```markdown
| Line | Keep/Cut/Rewrite | Novelty 1-10 | Intensity 1-10 | Reason | Revision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
```

## Mass-Market Clarity

Assume a smart but nontechnical reader with no category context.

Flag:

- Acronyms without context
- Abstract workflow claims
- Multiple concepts in one sentence
- Technical mechanism before concrete outcome
- Claims that require knowing the competitor landscape

Rewrite toward:

- Concrete nouns and visible actions
- One idea per sentence
- Before/after examples
- Familiar analogies only when accurate

## Evidence and Claim Integrity

Before finalizing, classify every concrete claim:

- **Source-backed**: product facts, metrics, customer outcomes, dates, competitive claims, roadmap status, pricing, integrations, and named customer statements.
- **Demo-backed**: claims directly proven by the product workflow or provided assets.
- **Creative framing**: metaphors, narrative contrast, category language, and emotional phrasing.
- **Assumption/placeholder**: useful but not yet verified.

Rewrite or label unsupported specifics. Creative framing may be bold, but it cannot invent facts.
